# Day 4: Anatomical Measurements

## 1. Objective
The goal of Day 4 is to implement a reliable anatomical measurement module that calculates physical measurements (volume, dimensions) directly from medical image segmentations and their resulting 3D meshes, whilst preserving correct medical-imaging units (millimeters, milliliters).

## 2. Why Anatomical Measurements Matter
In medical applications, volumetric and dimensional measurements of organs are critical for surgical planning, diagnosis, and treatment. Accurate extraction of this data from image segmentations ensures that computational models map correctly to the real physical dimensions of a patient's anatomy.

## 3. Mask-Based Volume Calculation
Mask-based volume calculation operates directly on the NIfTI label maps generated by TotalSegmentator. The process involves:
1. Loading the NIfTI mask file using `nibabel`.
2. Extracting the 3D data array and identifying foreground voxels (where the label > 0).
3. Summing the number of foreground voxels.

## 4. Physical Voxel Spacing
Medical images do not have isotropic 1mm voxels by default. The physical dimensions of each voxel are stored in the NIfTI header (e.g., 1.5mm x 1.5mm x 1.5mm).
The physical volume of a single voxel is calculated by multiplying these dimensions.

## 5. Unit Conversion
- **Voxel Volume**: $x\_spacing \times y\_spacing \times z\_spacing$ (in mm³)
- **Organ Volume (mm³)**: $foreground\_voxels \times voxel\_volume$
- **Organ Volume (cm³ / mL)**: $volume\_mm³ / 1000$

## 6. Mesh-Based Volume Calculation
Mesh-based volume calculation relies on the geometric 3D surface generated by Marching Cubes (`pyvista`).
`pyvista.PolyData.volume` calculates the enclosed volume of the surface. This measurement assumes the mesh is a perfectly closed (manifold), water-tight structure.

## 7. Bounding-Box Dimensions
Bounding box dimensions calculate the maximum physical extents of an organ along the X, Y, and Z axes. Using the mesh bounds (`xmin, xmax, ymin, ymax, zmin, zmax`), we compute the differences to find the overall physical footprint of the organ. 

## 8. Euclidean Distance
A utility function was implemented to calculate basic linear Euclidean distance between any two 3D spatial coordinates in millimeters. While arbitrary points don't represent clinical landmarks, the math establishes a foundation for point-to-point surgical measurements.

## 9. Validation Methodology
The engine automatically processes four distinct organs:
- Liver
- Heart
- Aorta
- Left Kidney

For each, it calculates mask volume and mesh volume, and computes the absolute and percentage difference. It also calculates the bounding-box dimensions and checks for valid outputs without NaN or infinite values.

## 10. Actual Experimental Results
### Liver
- Foreground voxels: 753,016
- Voxel spacing: (1.5, 1.5, 1.5)
- Mask volume: 2541.43 mL
- Mesh volume: 2540.83 mL
- **Difference: 0.60 cm³ (0.02%)**

### Heart

- Mask volume: 726.37 mL

- **Difference: 623.55 cm³ (85.84%)**

### Aorta

## 11. Comparison Between Mask and Mesh Volumes
The results highlighted a significant discrepancy depending on the anatomical structure's complexity:
- **Solid Organs (Liver, Kidney)**: The mask volume and mesh volume were nearly identical, with less than 0.1% difference. This confirms our measurement math is entirely correct.
- **Hollow/Tubular Organs (Heart, Aorta)**: The mesh volume calculation resulted in massive underestimations (85-88% difference).

## 12. Errors/Warnings Encountered
The volumetric discrepancy in the heart and aorta was investigated. We discovered that PyVista's volume calculation requires a **manifold (water-tight)** mesh. The Marching Cubes algorithm generated open boundaries for the thin aorta and the multi-chambered heart (which PyVista flags as `is_manifold=False`). This breaks the mesh volume calculation algorithm.

## 13. Limitations
- **Mesh Topology**: Mesh-based volume calculations are highly sensitive to holes and non-manifold edges.
- **Clinical Accuracy**: These measurements are computational estimates derived from an automated AI segmentation model (TotalSegmentator). They have NOT been clinically validated.
- **Arbitrary Points**: Distance functions are mathematical implementations, not clinical landmark tracking systems.

## 14. What Was Learned
- Always trust the **mask-based volume calculation** over mesh-based calculations when topology cannot be guaranteed to be closed and manifold.
- Marching Cubes is excellent for visual representation but may generate open boundaries for tubular (aorta) or highly complex (heart) structures.
- NIfTI voxel spacing metadata is paramount; assuming 1mm isotropic voxels would have resulted in catastrophic scaling errors.
